API Reference

CausalLoopDiagrams — Module
CausalLoopDiagrams

Causal loop diagrams as labelled graphs, and the bridge between them and the Bayesian networks of the ecorecipes ecosystem.

A causal loop diagram is what a group of people draw on a whiteboard when they first describe a system: factors joined by arrows, each arrow marked with the direction in which it pushes, and loops closing back on themselves. Baez formalises one as a graph whose edges are labelled by elements of a monoid of polarities, which makes a labelling the same thing as a functor from the free category on the graph into the delooping of the monoid (Baez, 2024); Baez and Chaudhuri take it further, to three kinds of morphism between labelled graphs – refining, simplifying and finding motifs – and to a homology that counts the feedback loops a composite acquires that neither part had (Baez and Chaudhuri, 2025).

A Bayesian network carries much more: a conditional probability table for every mechanism rather than a sign. It also carries less, in one specific way: it must be acyclic, so it cannot hold a loop at all.

This package is the two-way translation.

  • Polarity is the label: POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, NO_EFFECT and INDETERMINATE, a commutative rig under composition along a path (*) and combination in parallel (+).
  • polarity reads a polarity off a conditional probability table, by stochastic monotonicity in the declared order of the states. Monotone kernels compose, which is what makes the reading a labelling in Baez's sense rather than a convention: the polarity of a path really is the product of the polarities of its links.
  • causal_loop_diagram builds a diagram from a list of links – the sketch, drawn by hand, loops intact – or reads one off a BayesianNetworks.BayesModel, in either an interventional (:structural) or an observational reading.
  • loops, delayed_loops and instantaneous_loops enumerate the feedback and classify it. The distinction the last two draw is the one that matters for modelling: a loop with a delay in it is a loop a dynamic Bayesian network holds as it stands, the delayed link becoming a lagged input, and causal_loop_diagram recovers it by collapsing the time index of a transition template. A loop with no delay asserts simultaneity and no Bayesian network holds it.
  • coarsen, collapse_lags and compare_diagrams are the maps between diagrams: the push-forward of labels along a map of factors, its time-index special case, and a link-by-link comparison of two labellings of one graph.
  • occurrences and motif_census are the third of Baez and Chaudhuri's morphisms, finding motifs: a motif is itself a small labelled graph, an occurrence of it is a label-respecting embedding, and the census counts the shapes across a collection of diagrams. MOTIF_LIBRARY names the standard ones.

What the translation discards is worth stating plainly, because it is the reason a diagram is a sketch and a network is a model. A polarity cannot hold a magnitude, so it cannot say which of two loops through the same factor wins. It cannot hold an interaction: a link whose sign depends on another parent comes out INDETERMINATE, and a quantity that is worth more alongside an intervention than alone has no expression in the diagram at all. And it cannot hold a variable whose states have no order, which is what most decisions between incomparable options are.

Part of the ecorecipes compositional Bayesian-network ecosystem.

Polarities

CausalLoopDiagrams.Polarity — Type
Polarity

The label on a link of a CausalLoopDiagram: one of POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, NO_EFFECT and INDETERMINATE.

The four values are the subsets {0}, {+}, {-} and {0, +, -} of the sign hyperfield, and they form a commutative rig under its operations (Baez, 2024): * composes links along a path (so one(Polarity) == POSITIVE) and + combines links in parallel (so zero(Polarity) == NO_EFFECT). Negation is multiplication by NEGATIVE.

show prints the symbol (polarity_symbol), so a vector of polarities reads as a row of signs.

julia> POSITIVE * NEGATIVE
-

julia> NEGATIVE * NEGATIVE
+

julia> POSITIVE + NEGATIVE
?

julia> (one(Polarity), zero(Polarity))
(+, 0)

CausalLoopDiagrams.POSITIVE — Constant
POSITIVE

The polarity of a link along which the child moves the same way as the parent: raising the parent raises the child in the sense of stochastic dominance (see polarity). It is the multiplicative unit of Polarity (one(Polarity)) and prints as +.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.NEGATIVE — Constant
NEGATIVE

The polarity of a link along which the child moves the opposite way to the parent. Prints as -.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.NO_EFFECT — Constant
NO_EFFECT

The polarity of a link along which nothing moves: the table of the child does not depend on this parent at all. It is the additive unit of Polarity (zero(Polarity)) and absorbing for *, and prints as 0.

Baez's three-element monoid {+, 0, -} writes 0 for an indeterminate sign and has no element for this one; here the two are INDETERMINATE and NO_EFFECT respectively, because a conditional probability table tells them apart.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.INDETERMINATE — Constant
INDETERMINATE

The polarity of a link with no consistent direction: the child depends on the parent, but whether it rises or falls depends on the other parents, or on which pair of parent levels is compared. Prints as ?.

This is the value a genuine interaction takes, and it is the honest label for a link out of a variable whose states carry no order at all (a treatment with several incomparable options, say); causal_loop_diagram takes an unordered keyword that marks such variables.

CausalLoopDiagrams.polarity — Method
polarity(k::FiniteKernel, position::Integer; atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> Polarity
polarity(k::FiniteKernel, parent::Symbol; atol = DEFAULT_ATOL) -> Polarity

The polarity of the dependence of a kernel's child on one of its parents, named or given by its position in the domain: the direction in which the child's distribution moves as that parent rises through its states, with the other parents held fixed.

The test is stochastic monotonicity in the declared order of the states. Writing F(j | x) for the probability that the child is in state j or later given parent level x, the kernel is increasing in that parent when F(j | x) never decreases as x rises, for every j and every configuration of the other parents, and decreasing when it never increases. So

  • POSITIVE – increasing and not constant,
  • NEGATIVE – decreasing and not constant,
  • NO_EFFECT – the child's distribution does not depend on this parent,
  • INDETERMINATE – neither, which is what an interaction with another parent looks like.

atol is the slack allowed in each comparison; it defaults to FiniteKernels' DEFAULT_ATOL.

Monotone kernels compose, which is what makes this reading a labelling in the sense of (Baez, 2024): if the child's distribution rises with the parent and a further kernel is monotone in the child, the composite rises with the parent, so the polarity of a path is the product of the polarities of its links. That is a theorem, proved for one-parent kernels in exact arithmetic in proofs/CausalLoopDiagramsProofs/Theory/Composition.lean (reads_comp, and polarity_comp for the statement in the rig); the test suite checks the same identity numerically against compose_kernel on all four sign combinations, which is what stands behind the floating-point case and the atol slack below. The direction depends on the order the states were declared in; a variable whose states run from best to worst reads backwards, which is what the reversed keyword of causal_loop_diagram is for.

Both state orders matter, so a kernel over unordered states has no meaningful polarity; nothing here can detect that, and it is the caller's business to say so.

julia> X = FiniteAxis(:Rain, [:dry, :wet]);

julia> Y = FiniteAxis(:Grass, [:brown, :green]);

julia> polarity(cpt(X, Y, [0.9 0.1; 0.2 0.8]), :Rain)
+

julia> polarity(cpt(X, Y, [0.5 0.5; 0.5 0.5]), :Rain)
0

CausalLoopDiagrams.SchCausalLoop — Constant
SchCausalLoop

Schema of a causal loop diagram: objects Factor and Link, homs link_source: Link -> Factor and link_target: Link -> Factor, attribute types Label, Sign and Flag, and attributes factor_name (on Factor), link_polarity and link_delay (on Link). There are no path equations.

CausalLoopDiagrams.delayed — Function
delayed(spec) -> LinkSpec

Mark a link as crossing a delay: the effect of the source on the target is not felt in the same period. spec is a LinkSpec or a (source, target) => polarity pair.

The mark is what separates the two kinds of feedback loop. A loop with a delay in it is a loop a dynamic Bayesian network represents directly, the delayed link becoming a lagged input. A loop with no delay anywhere asserts that everything in it happens at once, and no Bayesian network, static or dynamic, can hold it without an extra assumption; see delayed_loops and instantaneous_loops.

CausalLoopDiagrams.link_polarity — Function
link_polarity(cld, source::Symbol, target::Symbol) -> Polarity

The polarity of the dependence of target on source: the sum, in the rig of Polarity, of the polarities of the parallel links between them. With no link at all the sum is empty and the answer is NO_EFFECT, the additive unit; use has_link to tell that case from a link that carries no effect.

Two parallel links of opposite sign give INDETERMINATE, which is the honest answer: the diagram says the two pathways disagree and says nothing about which wins.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.link_delay — Function
link_delay(cld, source::Symbol, target::Symbol) -> Bool

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.polarity_matrix — Function
polarity_matrix(cld) -> Matrix{Polarity}

The diagram as a square matrix over the rig of Polarity: entry (i, j) is the polarity of the link from factor i to factor j, in the order of factor_names, and NO_EFFECT where there is no link.

Matrix multiplication over this rig then does what it does for an ordinary adjacency matrix: the (i, j) entry of the n-th power is the sum of the polarities of the paths of length n from i to j, so a diagonal entry of a power is a loop polarity, summed over the loops of that length.

source

delay_matrix(cld) -> Matrix{Bool}

Loops

CausalLoopDiagrams.Loop — Type
Loop(factors, polarity, delay)

An elementary feedback loop: the factors in the order the loop visits them, starting from the one with the smallest part id, its polarity and whether it crosses a delay. The closing link runs from the last factor back to the first.

A loop whose polarity is POSITIVE is reinforcing: a push on any factor in it comes back as a push the same way. A NEGATIVE loop is balancing: the push comes back the other way. A loop whose polarity is INDETERMINATE is a loop whose direction the diagram does not determine, and one whose polarity is NO_EFFECT runs through a link along which nothing moves, so it is not live.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.loops — Function
loops(cld; max_loops = 1_000) -> Vector{Loop}

Every elementary feedback loop of the diagram: every directed cycle that visits no factor twice, shortest first. Parallel links are summed first (see link_polarity), so each cycle of factors appears once, and a self-link is a loop of length one.

Enumerating elementary cycles is exponential in the worst case; max_loops caps it and TooManyLoopsError says so.

julia> cld = causal_loop_diagram([(:Hare, :Lynx) => POSITIVE,
                                  delayed((:Lynx, :Hare) => NEGATIVE)]);

julia> only(loops(cld))
Hare -> Lynx -> Hare (-, balancing, delayed)

CausalLoopDiagrams.loop_polarity — Function
loop_polarity(cld, factors::AbstractVector{Symbol}) -> Polarity

The polarity of the loop that visits these factors in this order and closes from the last back to the first: the product of the polarities of its links. Throws NotALoopError if a factor repeats or a consecutive pair has no link, and UnknownFactorError if a name is not a factor of the diagram.

CausalLoopDiagrams.instantaneous_loops — Function
instantaneous_loops(cld; max_loops = 1_000) -> Vector{Loop}

The loops with no delay anywhere in them. These are the loops that assert simultaneous mutual dependence, and no Bayesian network holds one: a directed cycle in a belief network is rejected (BayesianNetworks.CyclicBayesNetError), and unrolling does not help, because there is no slice boundary to put the closing link across. Representing one means changing the model: separating the factor into stages, pushing it to equilibrium, or admitting a delay that the sketch left out.

From a Bayesian network

CausalLoopDiagrams.causal_loop_diagram — Method
causal_loop_diagram(m::BayesModel; mode = :structural, unordered = Symbol[],
                    reversed = Symbol[], atol = DEFAULT_ATOL, kwargs...)

The causal loop diagram of a model with its kernels bound: one factor per variable, in variable-id order, and one link per arc, labelled with the direction in which the arc moves (see polarity).

mode picks which question the labels answer.

  • :structural (the default) reads each mechanism's own table, one link per input, with the other parents held fixed. This is the interventional reading: the sign is what do_intervention on the parent would move. A variable wired to a mechanism twice gets two parallel links, which link_polarity then sums.
  • :observational reads conditional(m, child, parent) for each arc of the graph instead, so the sign is the direction in which the two move together, confounding and all. This enumerates the joint, so it is capped the way BayesianNetworks.conditional is capped; kwargs (for instance max_states, evidence) are forwarded to it and are otherwise unused.

The declared order of a variable's states is what "rises" means, so two keywords say where that order is not the quantity the diagram is about.

  • reversed names the variables whose states run the other way – declared best to worst when the diagram is about badness, say. Each reversed endpoint flips the sign of every link into and out of it.
  • unordered names the variables whose states carry no order at all: a platform choice, a delivery route, a species identity. Every link touching one is INDETERMINATE, which is the truth. A diagram over a decision with incomparable options has little to say, and saying so is the point.

Unbound mechanisms raise BayesianNetworks.MissingKernelError from kernel, unchanged.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.causal_loop_diagram — Method
causal_loop_diagram(dm::DynamicBayesModel; kwargs...) -> CausalLoopDiagram

The causal loop diagram of a dynamic model's transition template, with the time index quotiented away: one factor per template variable, a lagged input X[t-k] of Y becoming a delayed link from X to Y, and a same-slice input an ordinary one.

This is the direction that recovers the loops. A dynamic Bayesian network is a Bayesian network, so its transition template is acyclic and carries no loop; collapsing the slices puts the loops back, and the ones that come back are exactly the delayed ones. Persistence of a variable from one slice to the next becomes a self-link, which is the shortest feedback loop there is.

Equivalent to collapse_lags(causal_loop_diagram(transition_network(dm); kwargs...)); kwargs are those of causal_loop_diagram.

Maps between diagrams

CausalLoopDiagrams.coarsen — Function
coarsen(cld, f; combine = true) -> CausalLoopDiagram

The diagram pushed forward along a map of factors: f sends each factor name to the name it becomes, as a Dict (names it does not mention are kept) or as a function. Links follow their endpoints, keeping their polarity and their delay.

With combine = true (the default) links that become parallel are merged into one, their polarities added in the rig of Polarity – so two pathways that disagree become one INDETERMINATE link – and the merged link is delayed if any of them was. With combine = false they are left side by side, which keeps every pathway visible; link_polarity adds them on reading either way.

Merging two factors that were linked to each other turns that link into a self-link, which is a feedback loop of length one. That is not a defect of the operation: it is what coarsening a diagram does to the detail inside a factor.

julia> cld = causal_loop_diagram([(:Spores, :Infection) => POSITIVE,
                                  (:Conidia, :Infection) => POSITIVE]);

julia> lumped = coarsen(cld, Dict(:Spores => :Inoculum, :Conidia => :Inoculum));

julia> nfactors(lumped), nlinks(lumped)
(2, 1)
source
CausalLoopDiagrams.collapse_lags — Function
collapse_lags(cld) -> CausalLoopDiagram

The diagram with its time index quotiented away: a factor named X[t-k] becomes X, and a link that crosses a slice boundary becomes a delayed link. Names follow BayesianNetworks' lag convention, which lag_of parses.

This is the operation that turns a transition template back into a diagram with loops in it. The template is acyclic, as every Bayesian network must be; the loops are carried by the lags, and collapsing the index is what makes them visible again. A variable that persists from one slice to the next becomes a self-link.

Parallel links produced by the collapse are merged as in coarsen, so a variable that enters a mechanism both at the current slice and at a lag yields one link, delayed, whose polarity is the sum of the two.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.compare_diagrams — Function
compare_diagrams(a, b) -> DiagramComparison

Compare two diagrams link by link: which links both have and agree on, which both have and disagree on, and which only one has. Parallel links are summed first, so the comparison is between the polarities link_polarity reports.

Three uses, all of them checks that something has not been assumed.

  • A hand-drawn sketch against the diagram read off the finished network says which links the modelling kept, which it silently re-signed, and which feedback it had to cut.
  • The :structural against the :observational reading of one network (see causal_loop_diagram) says where the direction two variables move together is not the direction intervening on one moves the other. Those links are the confounded ones, and a diagram drawn from observed co-movement has them wrong while claiming to be causal.
  • A diagram against the collapse of a dynamic model's template (collapse_lags) says whether the loops the sketch drew are the loops the dynamic model closed.
source
CausalLoopDiagrams.DiagramComparison — Type
DiagramComparison

The result of compare_diagrams: four lists of links, each entry naming its endpoints.

  • agree: links both diagrams have, with the same polarity. Entries are (; source, target, polarity).
  • disagree: links both diagrams have with different polarities. Entries are (; source, target, first, second), the two polarities in the order the diagrams were given.
  • only_first, only_second: links only one diagram has, as (; source, target, polarity).

only_first is the interesting list when the first diagram is a sketch and the second was read off a network: it is what the network leaves out.

Motifs

Finding motifs is the third of Baez and Chaudhuri's morphisms between labelled graphs. A motif is itself a small labelled graph, and an occurrence of it is an embedding that respects the labels: the matching runs along the subset order on Polarity (issubset), so a motif link labelled INDETERMINATE matches a link of any sign.

CausalLoopDiagrams.occurrences — Function
occurrences(motif, cld; induced = false, match_delay = false,
            max_occurrences = 10_000) -> Vector{MotifOccurrence}

Every occurrence of motif in the diagram cld: every injective map from the motif's factors to the diagram's under which each link of the motif becomes a link of the diagram whose polarity the motif's label allows.

Both arguments are ordinary diagrams; a motif is a small labelled graph and nothing more, so causal_loop_diagram builds one, and motif names the standard shapes.

A label matches by containment rather than equality (issubset on Polarity): a motif link labelled INDETERMINATE asks only that some link be there, whatever its sign, while one labelled POSITIVE matches a positive link alone. A host link that is itself INDETERMINATE matches only a motif link that is, because the diagram does not claim a sign for it.

induced = false (the default) lets the diagram carry further links among the matched factors: the question is whether the shape is present. With induced = true the matched factors must carry exactly the motif's links and no others.

match_delay = false (the default) ignores delayed flags, because a motif is a question about shape; with match_delay = true each link must agree about its delay as well.

The map is injective, so distinct motif factors match distinct diagram factors: a chain of three does not occur in a loop of two. A motif with a symmetry occurs once per symmetry – :mutual_amplification has two occurrences in a single reinforcing pair, one for each way round – which is a property of counting maps, not a defect.

Throws TooManyOccurrencesError past max_occurrences.

julia> cld = causal_loop_diagram([(:Hare, :Lynx) => POSITIVE,
                                  (:Lynx, :Hare) => NEGATIVE]);

julia> only(occurrences(motif(:predator_prey), cld))
A = Hare, B = Lynx

Base.issubset — Method
issubset(a::Polarity, b::Polarity) -> Bool

Whether every sign a admits is a sign b admits, in the subset order the four polarities carry by construction: {0}, {+} and {-} are the three atoms, each contained in itself and in {0, +, -} (INDETERMINATE) and in nothing else.

It is a partial order, not a total one – POSITIVE and NEGATIVE are incomparable – with INDETERMINATE as its greatest element. There is no least element, because the empty set is not one of the four.

This is the order a motif matches along (see occurrences), and the direction matters: a motif link matches a diagram's link when issubset(diagram_label, motif_label), the label found being one of the signs the motif will admit. So a motif link labelled INDETERMINATE asks only that some link be there and matches whatever sign it carries, while one labelled POSITIVE matches a positive link alone. The wildcard of the motif search is therefore not an extra value added for it, but the top of the rig that was already there.

Read the other way the containment says something different and also true: a link which is genuinely INDETERMINATE does not match a motif asking for POSITIVE, because the diagram does not claim that sign.

julia> issubset(POSITIVE, INDETERMINATE)
true

julia> issubset(INDETERMINATE, POSITIVE)
false

julia> issubset(POSITIVE, NEGATIVE)
false

The standard shapes

CausalLoopDiagrams.MOTIF_LIBRARY — Constant
MOTIF_LIBRARY

The standard shapes, as an OrderedDict from name to diagram. motif takes a copy of one by name and motif_names lists them.

nameshapewhat it says
:self_reinforcingA -(+)-> Aa factor that feeds itself: growth, or decline
:self_balancingA -(-)-> Aa factor that damps itself
:mutual_amplificationA -(+)-> B -(+)-> Aeach raises the other; reinforcing
:mutual_suppressionA -(-)-> B -(-)-> Aeach suppresses the other; reinforcing too
:predator_preyA -(+)-> B -(-)-> Athe balancing pair
:chainA -(?)-> B -(?)-> Cany two links in series
:common_causeA -(?)-> B, A -(?)-> Cone factor driving two: the confounding shape
:common_effectA -(?)-> C, B -(?)-> Ctwo factors meeting at one
:opposed_pathwaystwo routes A to D, signs + + and + -the shape whose coarsening is INDETERMINATE
:agreeing_pathwaystwo routes A to D, both + +the same shape that coarsens to a sign

The three with ? labels are shape-only and match any sign (see occurrences); they are the ones to count rather than to read a direction off.

:mutual_suppression is in the library because it is the one practitioners misread: two negative links in series multiply to a positive loop, so mutual suppression is reinforcing, not balancing. :opposed_pathways and :agreeing_pathways are the same unlabelled shape with different signs, and they are worth telling apart because coarsen merges the first into an INDETERMINATE link and the second into a signed one: a sketch that lumps them loses a trade-off it cannot then state.

Canonical form and the census

CausalLoopDiagrams.motif_key — Function
motif_key(cld; match_delay = false) -> String

A string that is the same for two diagrams exactly when they are isomorphic as labelled graphs: the canonical form, found by minimising over every relabelling of the factors. The factor names themselves are discarded, which is the point – a motif is a shape.

The key reads as the links of the canonical labelling, whose factors are A, B, C, ... in canonical order, so "3: A+>B B->C" is a three-factor chain, positive then negative.

With match_delay = true delays are part of the shape, so the same signed shape with and without a delay gets different keys; a delayed link is marked ~.

Brute force over all relabellings, so it is for motifs: it throws an ArgumentError past seven factors.

julia> a = causal_loop_diagram([(:Rain, :Runoff) => POSITIVE]);

julia> b = causal_loop_diagram([(:Spores, :Infection) => POSITIVE]);

julia> motif_key(a) == motif_key(b)
true

julia> motif_key(a)
"2: A+>B"

CausalLoopDiagrams.canonical_motif — Function
canonical_motif(cld; match_delay = false) -> CausalLoopDiagram

The diagram relabelled into its canonical form: the same shape with its factors renamed A, B, C, ... so that two isomorphic diagrams come out identical. This is the representative motif_census reports, and motif_key is its string form.

With match_delay = false (the default) the delays are dropped, since they are not then part of the shape being counted.

CausalLoopDiagrams.motif_census — Function
motif_census(diagrams; size, match_delay = false, connected = true)
    -> Vector{MotifCount}

The shapes of size factors that occur across a collection of diagrams, counted by how many diagrams contain each: MotifCount rows, the widest support first.

diagrams is a Dict of name to diagram, a vector of name => diagram pairs, or a plain vector (named d1, d2, ... in order).

The census is induced, which is the opposite of occurrences's default and is deliberate: each set of size factors contributes exactly one shape, the one it actually carries, so the rows partition the sets counted and the occurrences column adds up. Were it not induced, a set carrying a loop would also be counted as each chain inside it, and a shape's count would say more about the vocabulary of shapes than about the diagrams.

connected = true (the default) keeps only those sets whose factors all touch a link inside the set and which hang together as an undirected graph. For size = 1 that leaves exactly the self-links, which is the useful one-factor question.

With match_delay = true a delay is part of the shape, so the same signed shape counts separately according to whether it crosses one.

What the result is for, and what it is not. support is the interesting column, and a shape supported by every diagram is usually telling you about the vocabulary rather than the subject – a bare link occurs everywhere. The counts are of factor sets, not of causal claims, and a polarity carries no magnitude, so a recurring shape is a question worth asking of each diagram rather than a finding on its own.

julia> a = causal_loop_diagram([(:X, :Y) => POSITIVE, (:Y, :X) => NEGATIVE]);

julia> b = causal_loop_diagram([(:P, :Q) => POSITIVE, (:Q, :P) => NEGATIVE]);

julia> only(motif_census([:a => a, :b => b]; size=2))
2: A+>B B->A  in 2 diagrams, 2 times

CausalLoopDiagrams.MotifCount — Type
MotifCount

One row of a motif_census: a shape, and where it was found.

  • key: the canonical form as a string (motif_key).
  • motif: the shape itself, canonically labelled (canonical_motif).
  • support: how many diagrams of the collection contain it. This is the column to read: a shape in one diagram is that diagram's business, and a shape in most of them is a property of the subject rather than of any one model.
  • occurrences: how many times in total, summed over the collection.
  • diagrams: the names of the diagrams that contain it, in the order given.

Exceptions

CausalLoopDiagrams.CausalLoopDiagramsError — Type
CausalLoopDiagramsError

Abstract supertype of every exception that CausalLoopDiagrams defines: UnknownFactorError, NotALoopError, TooManyLoopsError and TooManyOccurrencesError. It marks the package that introduced an error, not a kind of failure. Invalid arguments and keywords raise Base's ArgumentError instead, which is outside this root, and the typed errors of BayesianNetworks and FiniteKernels pass through unchanged.

CausalLoopDiagrams.TooManyLoopsError — Type
TooManyLoopsError(found, max_loops)

Thrown by loops when a diagram has more elementary loops than max_loops. Enumerating loops is exponential in the worst case, so the cap is a guard rather than a limit of the algorithm; raise it deliberately.

source
CausalLoopDiagrams.TooManyOccurrencesError — Type
TooManyOccurrencesError(found, max_occurrences)

Thrown by occurrences when a motif occurs in a diagram more often than max_occurrences. Subgraph matching is exponential in the size of the motif, so the cap is a guard rather than a limit of the algorithm; raise it deliberately.

A motif that occurs this often is usually a motif that says nothing – a bare link, or a chain of INDETERMINATE links, which matches everywhere.
