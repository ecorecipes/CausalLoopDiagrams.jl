CausalLoopDiagrams.jl

Causal loop diagrams as labelled graphs, and the bridge between them and the Bayesian networks of the ecorecipes ecosystem (CausalLoopDiagrams → BayesianNetworks → FiniteKernels).

A causal loop diagram is the first thing a group of people draw when they describe a system: factors joined by arrows, each arrow marked with the direction in which it pushes, and loops closing back on themselves. Baez formalises one as a graph whose edges are labelled by elements of a monoid of polarities, which makes a labelling the same thing as a functor from the free category on the graph into the delooping of the monoid (Baez, 2024); Baez and Chaudhuri extend this to three kinds of morphism between labelled graphs – refining, simplifying, finding motifs – and to a homology that counts the feedback a composite acquires that neither part had (Baez and Chaudhuri, 2025).

A Bayesian network carries much more than a sign per arrow, and one thing less: it must be acyclic, so it cannot hold a loop at all. This package is the translation in both directions.

The polarities

Polarity has four values, not the three of Baez's {+, 0, -}:

valueprintsmeaning
POSITIVE+the child moves the same way as the parent
NEGATIVE-the child moves the opposite way
NO_EFFECT0the child does not depend on the parent
INDETERMINATE?it depends which way, so the diagram does not say

The fourth and the third are distinct here because a conditional probability table tells them apart, where a whiteboard sketch cannot. The four are the subsets {0}, {+}, {-} and {0, +, -} of the sign hyperfield, closed under its multiplication and its multivalued addition, so they form a commutative rig: * composes links along a path and + combines links in parallel. The rig, rather than just a monoid, is what coarsen needs, because merging two factors must combine the labels of the links that become parallel.

Reading a sign off a table

polarity tests stochastic monotonicity in the declared order of the states: the child's distribution rises with the parent if its upper tail never shrinks, for every configuration of the other parents. Monotone kernels compose, which is what makes this a labelling in Baez's sense rather than a convention – the polarity of a path really is the product of the polarities of its links, and test/test_polarity.jl checks that against compose_kernel directly.

Two keywords say where the declared order is not the quantity the diagram is about: reversed for a variable whose states run best to worst, and unordered for one whose states carry no order at all, every link touching which comes out INDETERMINATE.

The two kinds of loop

using CausalLoopDiagrams

sketch = causal_loop_diagram([(:Inoculum, :Infection) => POSITIVE,
                              (:Infection, :Defoliation) => POSITIVE,
                              (:Defoliation, :Vigour) => NEGATIVE,
                              delayed((:Vigour, :Infection) => NEGATIVE),
                              delayed((:Infection, :Inoculum) => POSITIVE)])
loops(sketch)

delayed is the mark that matters for modelling, and it splits the feedback in two.

  • A loop with a delay in it (delayed_loops) is one a dynamic Bayesian network holds as it stands: the delayed link becomes a lagged input, and causal_loop_diagram recovers the loop from a transition template by collapsing the time index (collapse_lags). Persistence of a variable from one slice to the next comes back as a self-link, the shortest feedback loop there is.
  • A loop with no delay anywhere (instantaneous_loops) asserts that everything in it happens at once. No Bayesian network holds one, and unrolling does not help, because there is no slice boundary to put the closing link across. Representing one means changing the model.

What the translation discards

Worth stating plainly, because it is why a diagram is a sketch and a network a model. A polarity carries no magnitude, so it cannot say which of two loops through one factor wins – which is why Baez's series moves on to rings, hyperfields and rigs with more in them than signs. It carries no interaction: a link whose sign depends on another parent comes out INDETERMINATE, and a lever worth more alongside an intervention than alone has no expression in a diagram at all. And it carries no unordered variable, which is what most choices between incomparable options are.

Two readings of one network

causal_loop_diagram reads a model either way, and compare_diagrams says where they differ:

  • :structural reads each mechanism's own table, so the sign is what intervening on the parent would move;
  • :observational reads conditional(m, child, parent), so the sign is the direction in which the two move together in this model.

They part company exactly where the network is confounded. A causal loop diagram drawn from observed co-movement carries the second while claiming the first, so the disagreement list is a soundness check on a diagram someone else drew.
